Do You Qualify?

Florida law (Statute 381.986) lists specific qualifying conditions. A physician may also certify conditions "of the same kind or class" — so this list isn't the ceiling. Only a licensed Florida physician can determine if you qualify.

Chronic Nonmalignant Pain PTSD Cancer Epilepsy Glaucoma HIV / AIDS Crohn's Disease Parkinson's Disease Multiple Sclerosis ALS Terminal Conditions Anxiety (as comorbid) Depression (as comorbid) Conditions of Same Kind or Class
On anxiety and depression: These don't independently qualify in Florida. But if you have a listed qualifying condition — like PTSD or chronic pain — and also experience anxiety or depression alongside it, your physician can factor those in. Your doctor evaluates your full clinical picture.

What to Bring

Don't show up empty-handed. Having the right documents ready makes your appointment faster and your approval more straightforward.

  • Valid Florida driver's license or state ID — required to prove FL residency. If your ID shows an out-of-state or old address, bring a utility bill, lease agreement, or government mail showing your current FL address.
  • Medical records (if you have them) — not always required, but prior diagnoses, prescriptions, or test results make approval faster. A year or more of records is ideal. No records? You can still be evaluated — be ready to clearly describe your condition and symptoms.
  • Your phone or payment method — to pay for the eval at the clinic and to complete the state application online afterward.
  • Your OMMU login info (for renewals) — if you're renewing, you'll need access to the state registry portal. New patients receive login credentials by email after the physician submits your profile.

The Process — 5 Steps

  1. Book an in-person evaluation with a licensed Florida MMJ physician Florida law (HB 387, effective July 1, 2023) requires your first evaluation to be face-to-face — no exceptions. Any provider advertising 100% telehealth for new patients is not compliant with state law. Choose a certified physician, pick a location near you, and book online. Most initial visits take 10–30 minutes. See our provider comparison to find the cheapest option near you.
  2. Complete your evaluation — the doctor reviews your condition At your appointment, the physician reviews your medical history and the condition you're seeking certification for. If you qualify, they enter your information into the Florida Medical Marijuana Use Registry (MMUR) — usually the same day, sometimes within hours. Most reputable clinics offer a full refund if you don't qualify. Always confirm refund terms before booking.
  3. Get entered into the state registry — dispensary access starts now The moment your physician submits your certification to the MMUR, you can legally visit a dispensary. You do not need to wait for a physical card. OMMU will email you login credentials to complete your state application. You have 14 days from physician approval to submit — don't let it expire.
  4. Complete the OMMU application and pay the $75 state fee Log in to the OMMU portal using the credentials emailed to you. Upload proof of Florida residency and pay the mandatory $75–77.25 state fee. Your physical card arrives by mail in about 10 business days after OMMU approval. A digital version works at most dispensaries immediately — most patients shop before the card ever arrives in the mail.
  5. Renew every 210 days via telehealth Your physician certification must be renewed every 210 days (about 7 months). Your state card renews annually. Both must be active to legally purchase at dispensaries. After your initial in-person visit, every future renewal can be done entirely via video call from home. Set calendar reminders at 45 days out for both expirations — losing either one means losing dispensary access until it's renewed.

Common Questions

Can I get my Florida MMJ card fully online?
No — not for new patients. Florida law (HB 387, effective July 1, 2023) requires an in-person evaluation for your first certification. Only renewals are eligible for telehealth. Anyone advertising a fully online new-patient process is not compliant with state law.
What if I don't qualify?
Most reputable clinics — including DocMJ, My Florida Green, and Green Health Docs — offer a full refund if the physician determines you don't qualify. Ozark MMJ Cards does not publicly list a money-back policy. Always confirm refund terms before you book.
Does insurance cover any of this?
No. MMJ evaluations and cannabis products are not covered by any insurance in Florida. All costs are out of pocket.
Can I visit a dispensary before my physical card arrives?
Yes. Once your physician submits your certification to the MMUR, you have dispensary access. You don't need to wait for the physical card. Most patients shop for the first time within hours of their evaluation using the dispensary's registry lookup.
Can I use my Florida card in other states?
No. Florida MMJ cards are only valid in Florida. Florida does not participate in any cross-state MMJ recognition agreements. Transporting cannabis across state lines is a federal offense regardless of your card status.
Do I need to bring medical records?
Not always, but they help. Prior diagnoses, prescriptions, or test results make approval faster and more straightforward. If you don't have records, you can still be evaluated — just be prepared to describe your condition and symptoms clearly to the physician.
What's the difference between my physician cert and my state card?
Your physician certification is issued by the doctor who evaluated you and is valid for 210 days. Your state MMJ card is issued by the Florida OMMU and is valid for one year. Both must be active at the same time to legally purchase at dispensaries. They expire on different timelines — track both separately.
Does having an MMJ card affect my job or other rights?
Florida does not provide employment protections for MMJ cardholders. Employers can still require drug-free workplaces and test for cannabis. Federal employees and anyone subject to federal drug testing should consult a legal professional before applying. Cannabis remains federally controlled — housing and firearm purchase rules also apply.
